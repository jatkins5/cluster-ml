\suggestion{Consider adding a subsection titled ``Earlier merger activity
and learned predictions''. If adopted here, ``Additional diagnostics and
negative results'' would better describe the appendix: this experiment
finds a descriptive association but does not meet its criterion for
advancing to a new training target. Suggested text follows.

We tested whether the learned predictions were associated with merger
activity preceding the latest recorded collision. This separate
diagnostic used the Huber-loss X-ray models trained on catalogue time
since the latest first-pericentre event, capped at 4\,Gyr. For each
snapshot-99 parent halo, we averaged the five-seed ensemble predictions
over its nine simulated observations. Earlier activity was defined as
$M_{\rm prev}=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{P}}\mu_k
\exp(-\Delta t_k/\tau)$, where $\mu_k$ is the catalogue mass ratio,
$\Delta t_k$ is the elapsed time since event $k$, and $\tau=1$\,Gyr.
The set $\mathcal{P}$ contains only events strictly earlier than the
latest recorded collision; all events tied at the latest epoch were
excluded. The analysis included 308 haloes with finite latest-event
ages between 0 and 4\,Gyr and complete earlier-event weights. Of the
352 snapshot-99 haloes, six had no recorded past event, 37 had older
latest-event ages, and one had an invalid earlier-event mass ratio.

After adjustment for a fixed piecewise-linear time basis and original
cross-validation fold, greater earlier activity was associated with
lower predicted ages. The coefficient per unit $M_{\rm prev}$ was
$-1.170$\,Gyr, with a 95 per cent conditional bootstrap interval of
$[-1.858,-0.795]$\,Gyr, equivalent to $-0.141$\,Gyr per reference
standard deviation of activity. The in-sample partial $R^2$ was 0.059.
The association persisted with a prespecified natural-cubic time basis.
Joint adjustment for true halo mass, latest-group summed mass ratio and
latest-group multiplicity reduced the coefficient magnitude to
$-0.723$\,Gyr ($[-1.254,-0.476]$\,Gyr), on the same cohort.
Because these controls were added together, this attenuation cannot be
attributed to mass alone.

We separately tested whether earlier activity improved held-out tracking
of the learned output. Within each original CNN test fold, we divided
eligible parents into two fixed halves, fitted on one half and evaluated
on the other, then exchanged their roles. Neither half had been used to
fit or select the corresponding CNNs. Using the same piecewise-linear
time basis in both second-stage models, adding $M_{\rm prev}$ changed
held-out tracking $R^2$ from 0.126 to $-0.017$ across all 308 parents:
$\Delta R^2=-0.143$, with interval $[-0.482,+0.072]$.
These scores describe prediction of the CNN output, not recovery of
true collision time. All quoted intervals used 4000 parent bootstrap
draws stratified by original fold: the association fits were refitted
in each draw, whereas the held-out contrast used fixed second-stage
predictions. The intervals are conditional on the stored CNN outputs
and, for the held-out comparison, the fitted second-stage models;
they do not propagate CNN retraining uncertainty.

The prespecified criterion for advancing to activity-target training
required both the negative association to persist under the alternate
time basis and a positive held-out increment with its interval excluding
zero. The latter criterion was not met, so we did not advance to new
target training. These exploratory results support a conditional
association with catalogue-defined earlier activity, but do not identify
a causal contribution from earlier mergers or demonstrate improved
physical-time prediction. Conversely, the unresolved held-out increment
does not establish the absence of earlier-merger information. Catalogue
selection, the mass-ratio convention and history-correlated properties
such as halo mass remain limitations of the physical interpretation.}
