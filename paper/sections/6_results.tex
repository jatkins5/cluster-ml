\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{What the radio image knows}
\begin{itemize}
  \item All at 144\,MHz, snapshot 99, 5 seeds, same folds. Pooled CNN
        $0.485 \pm 0.028$ (ensemble 0.530) vs total flux 0.440, mass 0.314,
        five scalars 0.459; scalars + CNN 0.541, an increment of $+0.082$ (exploratory: the stack is not nested inside an outer split,
        so its optimism is unmeasured; a nested test is pending).
  \item Within mass terciles: CNN 0.33/0.33/0.24 while scalars fall to
        $-0.29$ in the high-mass bin; partial $\rho = 0.480$ at fixed mass.
  \item Morphology alone (flux-normalised maps): $0.414 \pm 0.040$, paired
        $-0.071$ against full flux ($p = 0.019$, 5/5 seeds lower); but
        shape and brightness are not separable (60\% of the shape-only
        model's output is predictable from the total flux it never saw).
  \item Caveat: scalars explain 73\% of the CNN output.
  \item Minkowski functionals as an interpretable baseline: XGBoost on 48
        topology numbers reaches 0.42 (cluster mean of the three
        projections; 0.30 per projection); fragmentation rises with merger
        age ($\rho = 0.62$ for the component count at the largest area
        fraction, i.e. the lowest intensity
        threshold).
  \item The same analyses at 1.4\,GHz differ by at most 0.02 (the
        high-mass tercile, 0.22 there).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{X-ray, and radio with X-ray}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The stretch bug and fix: X-ray 0.320 $\to$ 0.511 ($p = 0.0003$).
  \item Clean joint model on the 144\,MHz radio maps: $0.545$, $+0.059 \pm
        0.025$ over radio alone (paired seeds, $p = 0.007$, 5/5 seeds higher;
        seed-level evidence, not a halo-population interval). The earlier
        1.4\,GHz version gave 0.537.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Under observational degradation}
\label{sec:flux}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Forward models as corrected after review (Section~\ref{sec:fwd}):
        radio injected as linear emission with a flux-conserving rebin and
        one global flux constant fixed on total power (median matched to the
        Cuciti et al.\ 2023 relation; its per-cluster slope is not imposed);
        X-ray with the real data's count model (Cycle-22-equivalent exposure
        for focused photons, livetime for particles). Training on snapshots
        99 + 91; scores on the 352 snapshot-99 clusters
        (Table~\ref{tab:realistic}).
  \item Replacing the legacy forward model substantially reduces radio
        predictive performance, consistent with the removal of an artificial
        mass-dependent flux contribution (legacy realistic radio: 0.409,
        $\rho({\rm pred}, M) = -0.63$); the corrected 3\,Mpc configuration
        performs better than the corrected 1\,Mpc configuration. The box
        comparison also changes the injection placements.
  \item What the radio image adds beyond a noisy mass measurement (same
        clusters, seeds and fixed blurred-mass draw): $+0.099$ in
        single-observation $R^2$ (5-model ensembles; halo-bootstrap 95 per
        cent interval $[+0.039, +0.158]$). Descriptive: the two arms used
        different fold partitions, and the interval is conditional on the
        fitted models, those partitions and the mass draw.
  \item Adding radio to X-ray gives no demonstrated improvement: $+0.031$ in
        single-observation $R^2$, interval $[-0.014, +0.076]$, which does not
        exclude a modest gain (same descriptive qualification).
  \item X-ray is the strongest realistic input (single-observation 0.406).
        Its dependence on exposure is in Fig.~\ref{fig:xray_depth}, with a
        different (pooled, snapshot-99-only) estimator.
  \item The simulation's $P_{150}$--\mfive, from the cells with the density
        cut: slope $2.67\pm0.16$, scatter 0.78\,dex, consistent with the relic
        relation (de Gasperin et al.\ 2014, $2.83\pm0.39$) rather than the
        halo one (Cuciti et al.\ 2023, 3.55); scatter tracks merger state with
        the observed sign.
  \item Snapshot 91: an earlier paired comparison under the legacy forward
        models suggested gains from the second epoch for the noisiest inputs;
        it has not been repeated under the corrected forward models with
        common folds and is not used here. All realistic numbers above train
        on both epochs.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Loss, output head and contrastive pretraining}
\label{sec:loss_head}
\begin{itemize}
  \item In a matched exploratory comparison, replacing the Huber loss with
        MSE or a seven-class distributional head did not meet our
        prespecified point-prediction adoption criterion.
  \item The five-seed ensemble $R^2$ values were 0.432, 0.427 and 0.425,
        respectively; paired halo-bootstrap intervals for the latter two
        contrasts included zero.
  \item The distributional head improved categorical log score, ranked
        probability score and no-crossing Brier score relative to
        fitting-halo climatology, supporting its use as a probabilistic
        alternative without establishing calibration or equivalent point
        accuracy.
  \item The D4 contrastive pilot with a frozen encoder performed worse
        ($R^2 = 0.232$; $\Delta R^2 = -0.200$, conditional 95 per cent
        interval [$-0.260$, $-0.132$]).
  \item These results concern the capped proxy label and this fixed
        development protocol.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Idealised optical structure}
\label{sec:optical}
\begin{itemize}
  \item In an idealised optical experiment, adding magnitude-gap and
        positional-structure features to noisy mass, placement redshift and
        projected richness improved the five-seed single-observation
        ensemble from $R^2 = 0.313$ to 0.422 (paired $\Delta R^2 = 0.109$;
        95 per cent halo-bootstrap interval [0.048, 0.167]).
  \item With X-ray images also supplied, adding the same features changed
        $R^2$ from 0.488 to 0.469 ($\Delta R^2 = -0.019$; interval
        [$-0.067$, 0.029]), providing no demonstrated incremental
        improvement under the tested model and evaluation protocol.
  \item These features use simulation-defined FoF membership, true \rfive\
        and an idealised hot-gas centre; the experiment therefore tests
        idealised combined-input prediction rather than performance with
        observed optical catalogues.
  \item The intervals resample parent halos and are conditional on the
        fitted models.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Application to LoVoCCS}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Predictions for 26 (radio, 3\,Mpc cutouts) and 15 (X-ray) clusters;
        15 have both.
  \item Radio (3\,Mpc) and X-ray agree, using 25-model ensembles (5 folds
        $\times$ 5 seeds): Spearman $0.61$ ($p = 0.015$, $n = 15$); partial at
        fixed $z$ 0.71 (Freedman--Lane permutation $p = 0.005$); at fixed
        weak-lensing mass 0.54 ($n = 11$, $p = 0.11$). The target set differs
        from the earlier comparison (15 rather than 14 clusters).
  \item The scales do not agree: radio predictions are older than X-ray for
        14 of 15 clusters, by a median 0.44\,Gyr (medians 1.10 and
        0.83\,Gyr). Neither forward-model correction removed the offset; its
        cause is not identified, so absolute ages from either model should
        not be interpreted.
  \item A401 is the youngest for radio (1 of 26), radio + mass and mass
        alone (1 of 18); it is also the heaviest target with a weak-lensing
        mass, so its rank is mass-driven. A399 is youngest for X-ray (1 of
        15) and fifth for radio. Neither is presented as validation.
  \item Weak-lensing peak check: only 9 overlapping clusters,
        inconclusive.
\end{itemize}
