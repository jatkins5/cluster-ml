\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{What the radio image knows}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Pooled CNN $0.487 \pm 0.033$ (ensemble 0.528) vs total flux 0.432,
        mass 0.314, five scalars 0.455.
  \item Within mass terciles: CNN 0.33/0.32/0.22 while scalars fall to
        $-0.28$ in the high-mass bin; partial $\rho = 0.475$ at fixed mass.
  \item Morphology alone (flux-normalised maps): 0.418; but shape and
        brightness are not separable (shape-only model 59\% predictable
        from the flux it never saw).
  \item Caveat: scalars explain 72\% of the CNN output.
  \item Minkowski functionals as an interpretable baseline: XGBoost on 48
        topology numbers reaches 0.41 (cluster mean of the three
        projections; 0.30 per projection); fragmentation rises with merger
        age ($\rho = 0.62$ for the component count at the highest
        threshold).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{X-ray, and radio with X-ray}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The stretch bug and fix: X-ray 0.320 $\to$ 0.511 ($p = 0.0003$).
  \item Joint model $0.537 \pm 0.016$: beats radio by $+0.050$ and X-ray by
        $+0.026$; gains on older mergers (RMSE $>2$\,Gyr 1.25 $\to$ 1.13).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Under observational degradation}
\label{sec:flux}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Anchored vs simulation-power flux (1.4\,GHz, snapshot 99):
        0.338 $\to$ 0.412; image beyond mass $+0.079 \to +0.151$;
        $\rho({\rm pred}, M)$ $-0.89 \to -0.61$.
  \item The simulation's $P_{150}$--\mfive, from the cells with the density
        cut: slope $2.67\pm0.16$, scatter 0.78\,dex. Compare the relic
        relation (de Gasperin et al.\ 2014, $2.83\pm0.39$), not the halo
        one (Cuciti et al.\ 2023, 3.55). Scatter tracks merger state with
        the observed sign. (The earlier 3.30 / 1.65\,dex came from an image
        approximation.)
  \item Realistic pipeline at 144\,MHz, with and without snapshot 91
        (Table~\ref{tab:realistic}). Adding the earlier epoch helps where
        the training images are noisiest -- X-ray at real depths
        significantly -- and not at all for the clean radio CNN.
        Snapshot-91 clusters are themselves harder (0.35--0.43).
        $\rho({\rm pred}, M)$ strengthens slightly with snapshot 91
        ($-0.62 \to -0.64$ image only; $-0.70 \to -0.75$ with mass).
        \todo{More seeds for the joint and transfer rows (3 so far).}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Application to LoVoCCS}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Predictions for 25 (radio), 15 (X-ray), 14 (joint) clusters; all
        within the label range.
  \item Radio and X-ray agree (models trained on snapshots 99 + 91, 14
        clusters): $\rho = 0.72$ ($p = 0.003$), 0.72 at fixed $z$; at fixed
        weak-lensing mass 0.50 ($n = 10$, $p = 0.14$). Snapshot 99 alone
        gave 0.64 / 0.69 / 0.52.
  \item Adding snapshot 91 keeps the real clusters' rank order
        ($\rho = 0.86$--0.96 against the snapshot-99 models) but shifts them
        0.1--0.3\,Gyr younger on average.
  \item A399/A401 youngest -- but mass-driven; do not present as
        validation. \todo{Recheck with the 99 + 91 models.}
  \item Weak-lensing peak check: only 9 overlapping clusters,
        inconclusive.
\end{itemize}
