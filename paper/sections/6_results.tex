\subsection{Radio-only}

The 144 MHz radio images contain information beyond the realistic baselines considered here.
On the 352 clusters imaged at snapshot-99, the CNN that pooled all three orthogonal projections achieved $R^2 = 0.498 \pm 0.028$.
\stale{The previously reported five-seed ensemble score of 0.530 and the following scalar, mass-tercile and partial-correlation diagnostics use the previous radio prescription; their replacements are not in the reviewed results.}
This is compared to $R^2 = 0.314$ from ridge regression on the halo mass, 0.440 using integrated radio emission, and 0.459 using all five scalar features.
Combining the scalar features with the CNN gave $R^2 = 0.541$, an increment of 0.082 over the scalar model.

The CNN remained predictive within each mass tercile, with $R^2$ values of 0.332, 0.328, and 0.239 in the lowest, medium, and highest mass bins, respectively.
In comparison, the five-scalar model achieved $R^2=$ 0.295, 0.250, and $-0.287$.
The partial rank correlation between pseudo-TSC and the CNN predictions after controlling for halo mass was 0.480.
Thus, as the CNN achieved greater $R^2$ for each tercile, its performance is not explained solely by the overall association between mass and pseudo-TSC.
However, the scalar features predicted the CNN prediction with $R^2=0.732$, and thus they have substantial overlap.

Normalizing each radio projection to a common integrated emission value reduced the mean CNN score to $R^2=0.413 \pm 0.020$.
Performance decreased in all five random seeds, with a mean paired change of $-0.085\pm0.043$ and a paired-seed p-value of $0.012$.

\stale{The following Minkowski-functional results use the previous radio prescription and have not been regenerated.}
Part of the signal could also be recovered from a representation of the morphology.
An XGBoost decision tree model applied to 48 Minkowski-functional measurements predicted pseudo-TSC at $R^2 = 0.304$ for individual projections, and at $R^2 = 0.415$ after averaging predictions across all three cluster projections.
At the lowest intensity threshold, selecting approximately the brightest 50\% of pixels, the component count had Spearman correlation $\rho=0.62$ with pseudo-TSC.
Older proxy ages were thus associated with more fragmented emission.
Repeating the analysis at 1.4 GHz changed the scores by no more than 0.02.

\subsection{X-ray and Combined Model}

Clean X-ray images provided comparable predictive power to clean radio images.
Applying the globally-scaled arcsinh scaling increased the X-ray CNN correlation from $R^2=0.320$ to $0.511 \pm 0.026$.
Improvement was found in all five seeds, with a paired-seed p-value of $0.0003$.
This shows a large sensitivity to the preprocessing method.
The original X-ray count rates are much smaller than unity, so applying the arcsinh-stretch leaves most pixels nearly unchanged.
Using $\operatorname{arcsinh}(x/a)$ with a global scale $a$ compresses bright cores and increases the relative visibility of faint spatial structure, while preserving overall brightness differences between clusters.

Combining the stretched X-ray images with the 144 MHz radio maps yields a mean $R^2 = 0.535\pm0.020$.
The mean paired increment over radio alone was $0.037\pm0.032$, with improvement in all five seeds (paired-seed $p=0.064$).
This supports the hypothesis that there is complementary information in the clean radio and X-ray inputs.

\subsection{With Forward-Modelling}
\label{sec:flux}

Post-processing the radio images with a forward model substantially reduced the information that could be extracted.
Results in table \ref{tab:realistic} are obtained with radio emission cropped and rebinned in linear units with flux conservation, while X-ray photon sampling follows the exposure conventions of the processed observations.

For single projections, the radio-only ensemble of five models achieved $R^2 = 0.073$ with a box size of 1 Mpc, and $R^2=0.196$ with a box size of 3 Mpc.
The corresponding scores for the pooled input models, averaged over the different seeds, were $0.131\pm0.024$ and $0.219\pm0.023$.

To test sensitivity of the brightness contrast of the simulated radio emission, we applied an arcsinh compression to the source profile, then rescaled it to retain the same total physical flux before beam convolution and background injection.
This reduces the relative contribution of the brightest regions compared to fainter regions.
This injected source modification is separate from the final $\operatorname{arcsinh}(S/\sigma_{\rm rms})$ preprocessing applied to all inputs.
While the models predicting on larger boxes performed better, injection placements were also changed, so the effect of field size was not completely isolated.

The new radio/mass comparison and the separate 3 Mpc joint/X-ray comparison use matching outer folds and inner-selection halos between conditions.
The mass comparison also uses the same fixed noisy-mass measurements in both conditions and across seeds.
The 1 Mpc joint/X-ray comparison reuses an older X-ray reference with different folds and is therefore descriptive.
All bootstrap intervals remain conditional on the fitted models.

Despite significantly decreased performance, the radio images retained useful information beyond noisy mass measurements.
The mass-only model achieved a single-observation ensemble score of $R^2 = 0.176$, which increased to $0.305$ when the 3 Mpc radio image was also provided.
The paired difference was $+0.129$, with a $95\%$ interval of $[+0.068, +0.190]$.

X-ray images showed much stronger performance under forward-modelling, achieving a single-observation ensemble $R^2 = 0.406$.
The joint model using radio images with 1 Mpc box size scored $0.426$, a descriptive difference of $+0.020$ from that reference.
The $95\%$ interval was $[-0.032, +0.074]$.

In the separate matched comparison using 3 Mpc radio images, the X-ray control scored $R^2=0.403$ and the joint model scored $0.401$.
The paired difference was $-0.002$, with a $95\%$ interval of $[-0.057,+0.057]$.
\suggestion{Use this matched comparison for the main inference about
adding radio to X-rays: No improvement was resolved for the tested
3\,Mpc joint model. The interval does not establish equivalence or rule
out useful gains under other conditions. Keep the 1\,Mpc descriptive
comparison separate. The older mass-only baseline also used different
noisy inputs, so its change cannot be attributed to folds alone.}

We separately compared pooled-view and single-view X-ray training using the same persistent parent-halo folds, identical inner-selection halos, and single-view checkpoint scoring.
Pooled-view training achieved ensemble $R^2=0.425$ and MAE 0.649 Gyr, compared with $R^2=0.421$ and MAE 0.659 Gyr for single-view training.
The paired $R^2$ difference was $-0.004$ (95 per cent interval $[-0.027,+0.019]$), providing no evidence of improvement.
This protocol differs from the group-fold construction and pooled-view checkpoint selection used in Table~\ref{tab:realistic}, so the scores constitute a separate comparison.

In a separate exposure experiment, the mean pooled-view $R^2$ increased from 0.312 at 5 ks to 0.365 at 20 ks and 0.450 at 50 ks, with smaller subsequent increases to 0.476 at 150 ks and 0.499 at 500 ks.
Exposures are in Cycle-22-equivalent units.
This experiment used three seeds and trained only on snapshot-99 data, unlike the five-model, two-epoch single-observation results in Table~\ref{tab:realistic}.
It measures depth sensitivity within the background model, but doesn't isolate the benefit of adding snapshot-91 data.

\stale{The following regression-bias magnitudes and Figure~\ref{fig:tsc_pred} have not been updated for the regenerated radio models.}
Figure \ref{fig:tsc_pred} shows that the recovered age range is systematically compressed for the forward-modelled data.
The youngest mergers are predicted about 0.35 Gyr too old, while systems at the 4 Gyr maximum were predicted about 1.2-1.4 Gyr below the maximum.

\stale{The following power--mass fits and Figure~\ref{fig:pm} use the previous radio prescription; their slopes, scatter and residual correlation await remeasurement.}
The simulated radio power also varied systematically with the merger state and the mass.
After applying the density cut, the total 144 MHz power had a fitted mass slope of $2.67\pm0.16$ and scatter of $0.78$ dex.
Restricting the emission to the 3 Mpc injection box resulted in a slope of $2.36\pm0.19$ and scatter of 0.93 dex.
Residuals from the total-power relation decreased with pseudo-TSC, with Spearman $\rho=-0.33$.
Thus, recently disturbed systems tend to be brighter at a fixed mass.
The normalization was set globally, anchored to the observed halo-power relation; the simulated slope was not imposed.
Comparisons with observed halo and relic relations remain contextual, as frequency, source definition, fitting method, and selection differ, and the slope alone does not establish agreement with one particular source population.

\subsection{Loss, output head and contrastive pretraining}
\label{sec:loss_head}

We also tested alternative losses and output representations in an exploratory X-ray experiment.
The Huber loss baseline achieved a five-seed single-projection ensemble $R^2 = 0.432$ and MAE of 0.650 Gyr.
Using the mean squared error loss instead gave $R^2 = 0.427$ and MAE of 0.663 Gyr.
The seven-class distributional head gave $R^2 = 0.425$ with MAE of 0.666 Gyr.
Their paired $R^2$ differences from the Huber loss case were $-0.005$, with $95\%$ confidence interval of $[-0.029, +0.021]$ and $-0.007$ with confidence interval $[-0.034, +0.025]$ respectively.
Neither alternative yielded an increase of at least 0.02 in a single-view ensemble $R^2$, with an increase in MAE of less than or equal to 0.02 Gyr relative to the Huber loss control; this was our prespecified adoption rule before running the comparison.

However, the distributional head provided useful probabilistic predictions relative to the class frequencies among fitting halos.
Its categorical log score was 1.649 compared with 1.888 for the reference.
Its normalized ranked probability score was 0.125 compared with 0.160.
Its Brier score for the no-crossing category was 0.068 compared with 0.088.
All three paired conditional intervals favored the model.
The images therefore provide probabilistic information about the pseudo-TSC classes beyond the fitting-sample frequencies.

A pilot study using contrastive pretraining showed that this method performed significantly worse than the Huber loss control.
Rotations and reflections were applied to individual observations to construct positive pairs, followed by an encoder with weights frozen during downstream training.
This yielded $R^2=0.232$ and MAE of 0.768 Gyr.
The paired changes were $-0.200$ in $R^2$, with confidence interval $[-0.260, -0.132]$ and change in MAE of $+0.118$ and interval $[+0.074, +0.162]$ Gyr.
This result provides evidence against using the tested frozen-encoder configuration, though other contrastive objectives, or fine-tuning the encoder, could change the performance.

\subsection{Idealised optical structure}
\label{sec:optical}

Idealized optical structure supplied predictive information in addition to the scalar control model.
Adding magnitude-gap and positional-structure features to the noisy mass, placement redshift, and projected richness increased the single-projection ensemble $R^2$ from 0.313 to 0.422 and reduced MAE from 0.729 to 0.655 Gyr.
The paired $R^2$ increase was $0.109$, with $95\%$ confidence interval of $[+0.048, +0.167]$.
In the same experiment, adding X-ray images to the controls increased $R^2$ to 0.488, an increment of $+0.176$ with confidence interval $[+0.108, +0.243]$ and MAE of 0.605 Gyr.

However, adding the optical structure features to the X-ray images and controls did not demonstrate a further improvement, with $R^2=0.469$ and MAE of 0.632 Gyr, and paired $R^2$ change of $-0.019$ with confidence interval $[-0.067, +0.029]$.
These features use simulation-defined membership, true $R_{500}$, and an idealized hot gas reference center.
Thus, this experiment measures performance only with idealized combined inputs, rather than performance achievable from true optical catalogues.

\subsection{Application to LoVoCCS}
\label{sec:lovoccs}

Finally, we applied our predictive models to LoVoCCS clusters, obtaining radio predictions for 26 systems, and X-ray predictions for 15.
All 15 X-ray systems were present in the 3 Mpc radio sample; the 1 Mpc joint input sample contains 14 systems.
Each observed prediction averaged 25 models spanning five folds and five seeds.
Among the systems with both data sources, radio and X-ray predictions had Spearman $\rho = 0.67$ ($p=0.006$).
After controlling for redshift, the partial-rank correlation was 0.74, with a Freedman-Lane permutation $p=0.003$.
Controlling instead for weak-lensing mass gave a partial correlation of 0.58 among the 11 systems with available masses, with $p=0.078$.
This latter comparison does not establish agreement independent of mass.

Radio assigned older proxy ages to 14 of the 15 systems, with a median paired offset of 0.49 Gyr.

The origin of this offset remains unidentified.
The correlated rankings show cross-band consistency, but the age scales are not interchangable, and neither has been validated against independent constraints on the merger time.
