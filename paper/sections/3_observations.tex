Our observational sample is a subset of 26 clusters from the Local Volume-Complete Cluster Survey (LoVoCCS).
LoVoCCS contains 107 nearby, X-ray-luminous galaxy clusters between $z \in [0.03, 0.12]$ and with $L_{X,\text{500c}} > 10^{44}$ erg s$^{-1}$ in the 0.1-2.4 keV band.
Our subset was selected to have available radio imaging and, where available, archival Chandra observations and weak-lensing mass measurements from LoVoCCS.
Following these restrictions causes our subsample to lose the completeness of the full LoVoCCS survey \citep{Fu2024}.
Our 26-cluster sample spans $z \in [0.0353, 0.1198]$.

Radio images are from the third data release of the LOFAR Two-metre Sky Survey (LoTSS DR3), at a reference frequency of 144 MHz.
We extract squares of physical side length 3 Mpc from 80' cutouts centered on the catalogue coordinates.
We then resample the squares onto 128x128 pixel grids.
The native restoring beam size varies across targets, with 9" for 22 targets and 6" for four targets.
LoTSS DR3 observations below 10$^\circ$ declination have a 9" resolution, compared to 6" resolution above 10$^\circ$ declination.
Thus, the native restoring beam size varies across our sample, with 4/26 targets using the lower resolution of the $<10^\circ$ sample.
Our processing adopts a common 9" beam assumption for masking and mock construction.

To suppress contamination from compact sources, we identify connected components of emission above five times the measured root-mean-squared (rms) luminosity level.
We classify their compactness using the size of the area that is above their half their peak intensity, relative to the assumed beam half-power area.
Components occupying at most ten such beam areas are masked by replacing them with the surrounding annular median emission value with added Gaussian noise at the measured rms level.
The same procedure is applied to the field-injected mocks.
The resulting images are then ``arcsinh-stretched'', by applying $\operatorname{arcsinh}(S/\sigma_\text{rms})$, where $S$ is the pixel value and $\sigma_\text{rms}$ is a scale estimated by sigma-clipping before masking \todo{clarify this more}.
The median value of the scale on the resampled 3 Mpc cutouts is 0.143 mJy per beam.

The archival Chandra dataset contains 55 public observations of 19 of our radio targets.
We use the 25 ACIS-I observations, which cover 15 targets, matching the detector configuration of our simulated observations.

We combine all events in the 0.1-2 keV band for each target.
Exposure maps from the CIAO software evaluated at 1.2 keV are converted to Cycle-22-equivalent exposures.
These range in exposure time from 6.2-229.4 ks, with a median of 31.3 ks.
This approximately corrects for differences in effective area between observations.

We estimate the particle background from the 9.5-12 keV events \todo{explain why that range}, which we subtract before normalizing the exposure, then restore the the reference particle background level from the mocks.
X-ray images are centered on the smoothed emission peak within 2' of the catalog position, then bin the emission onto a 128x128 pixel grid, each pixel being about 18.9 kpc on a side.
We retain a circular aperture with approximate radius 485 kpc at $z \geq 0.05$, which we reduce at lower redshifts to remain within the 8' angular aperture.
Pixels outside the aperture are set to 0.
Chip gaps are filled by interpolation from adjacent pixels.
We arcsinh-stretch the resulting images with the same scale as the mocks.
Because the radio and X-ray images are centered differently, their relative positional offsets are not preserved.
Section \ref{sec:fwd} describes the count model and remaining footprint limitations.

LoVoCCS provides weak-lensing mass estimates for 18 of the 26 radio targets, including 11 of the 18 Chandra targets.
We use single-peak Navarro-Frenk-White (NFW) profile fits from the LoVoCCS processing outputs, and convert the $M_\text{200c}$ to $M_\text{500c}$ values using the NFW concentration relation and cosmology adopted in those fits.
The median fractional uncertainty on the input $M_\text{200c}$ measurements is about 23\%. \todo{probably should put exact number here}
These masses are used for the mass-conditioned analysis, as well as comparisons at fixed mass values.
Because the observed clusters lack independent merger-time labels, agreement between their radio and X-ray predictions is used to check consistency.
