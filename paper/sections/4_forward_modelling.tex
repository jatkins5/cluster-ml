\section{Forward modelling}
\label{sec:fwd}

\subsection{Radio LoTSS forward model}

We recovered the linear emission values from the arcsinh-stretched radio images, and then cropped and rebinned the data onto a 128$\times$128 pixel grid using pixel-overlap weights which conserved the integrated emission.
The radio-only inputs to our predictive models cover square regions of physical side length 3 Mpc, corresponding to 23.4 kpc per pixel.
This aperture retains 65\% of the uncropped emission, while the central 1 Mpc section only retains 4\%.
\stale{These aperture-retention fractions were measured with the previous radio prescription; their regenerated-model values are pending.}

The radio power assigned to each image is proportional to its integrated linear emission:
\begin{align*}
    P_\text{box}
    &= A_\text{rad} \sum_{p \in \text{box}} w_p
\end{align*}
where $w_p$ is the total emission weight in pixel $p$.
We set $A_\text{rad}$ by matching the median logarithmic total power of the full snapshot-99 maps to the median logarithmic power predicted at their cluster masses using thr relation in \cite{Cuciti2023}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:power-relation}
    \log_{10} \! \left( \frac{P_{150}}{10^{24.5}\, \text{W Hz}^{-1}} \right)
    = 1.1 + 3.55 \log_{10}\! \left( \frac{M_{500}}{10^{14.9} M_\odot} \right).
\end{equation}
The same constant is applied to every cluster in both snapshots, transferring the 150 MHz radio-halo power scale to the 144 MHz shock-emission maps.
Flux densities are calculated using the luminosity distance and a spectral index of $\alpha = 1.2$, defined by $S_\nu \propto \nu^{-\alpha}$ \citep{Cuciti2023}.

For each simulated cluster, we generated three observational realizations, drawing a redshift value from the radio target sample and independently selecting a background from 201 80' LoTSS DR3 fields.
Background positions were sampled 1-3 degrees from the survey targets; usable 80' fields were cropped to the angular size appropriate for the selected redshift and then resampled.
We draw real redshifts to ensure we sample from the observed redshift distribution.
Using real background fields supplies realistic observed noise correlations, unrelated radio sources, and imaging artifacts.
Each realization consists of the three orthogonal simulation projections, which had the same forward-modelling process applied.
We convolved the simulated emission maps with a circular 9" Gaussian restoring beam; the four observed targets with native 6" beams retained that resolution.
We converted the output to Jy per beam, and added this to the corresponding resampled field.
After injection, we apply the compact-source masking and arcsinh-stretch transformation used for observations (section~\ref{sec:obs}), estimating $\sigma_\text{rms}$ before masking each injected image.

Realism of the simulated images was evaluated using area fraction, perimeter, connected-component count, and Euler characteristic at ten logarithmically-spaced thresholds between 1 and $20\sigma_\text{rms}$.
We measured morphology agreement as
\newcommand{\median}{\operatorname{median}}
\begin{equation}
    D = \median_{j,k} \left\{\frac{\left|\median_i\left(F^\text{obs}_{ijk}\right) - \median_i\left(F^\text{mock}_{ijk}\right)\right|}{\sigma_i\left(F^\text{mock}_{ijk}\right)} \right\}
\end{equation}
where $F_{ijk}$ is the collection of values of statistic $j$ measured at threshold $k$ for image $i$, for the observed or mock maps.
$\sigma_i(F)$ is the standard deviation of $F$ over all images.
% Source: logs/syn_inject_8296721.out, radio-realism summary.
For the 3 Mpc-sized inputs, this was 0.093, compared with 0.159 for an alternative method using arcsinh-compressed emission morphology at the same integrated flux, and 0.635 for background-only fields.
The comparison includes 3168 mocks and 26 real observations, with the mocks each containing three projections and three realizations of the 352 source clusters.
This result supports the adopted processing at the level of morphological summaries.
However, since our source injection is performed in the image plane, it does not measure the recovery of diffuse flux through interferometric sampling and imaging.
Measuring this precisely would require visibility-domain injection, in e.g. \cite{Bruno2023}.

\subsection{X-ray Chandra forward model}
\label{sec:xray}

We degraded the 2 Ms ACIS-I Cycle-22 mocks described in section~\ref{sec:sims} to approximate the redshifts and observing depths of the Chandra targets.
The images cover the observed 0.1-2.0 keV band on a common physical grid, with pixel size of approximately 18.9 kpc.
The X-ray images inherit their redshift values from the corresponding simulated radio observations.

In cases when a true ACIS-I target exists at the assigned redshift, we use the exposure properties of that target; otherwise, those of a randomly-selected ACIS-I target are used.

Source counts are reduced by binomial thinning, with retainment probability:
\begin{equation}
    p_\text{src}
    = \min\! \left[1,\,
    \frac{T_\text{eq}}{2\,\text{Ms}}
    \left( \frac{D_L(z_\text{mock})}{D_L(z)} \right)^2
    f_{L_X} f_{N_H}
    \right]
\end{equation}

Here, $T_\text{eq}$ is the target's Cycle-22-equivalent exposure, and 2 Ms is the original mock exposure.
$D_L$ is the luminosity distance, $z$ is the assigned observing redshift, and $z_\text{mock}$ is the original observing redshift (0.05 for snapshot-99 and 0.0994 for snapshot-91).
The dimensionless factors $f_{L_X}$ and $f_{N_H}$ correct source brightness and Galactic absorption, respectively, as described below.
This placement approximates redshift through count scaling.

Detected photons can come from either the source and sky background, or from instrumental particles.
The former counts scale with the Cycle-22-equivalent exposure, $T_\text{eq}$, while the latter scale with detector livetime $T_\text{live}$.
$T_\text{eq} / T_\text{live}$ ranges from 0.85 to 1.83 for the observed targets.
Sky backgrounds include galactic foreground emission and cosmic X-rays, including from point sources.
Background counts per physical pixel are scaled with the angular area subtended by that pixel.
After thinning the input counts, we added independently sampled sky-background counts, and increased the particle background where needed to match the expected particle contribution for the target's livetime and the pixel's angular area.

Mock count rates follow the same subtraction and reference-background convention as the real observations:
\begin{align*}
    R
    = \frac{C-\hat P}{T_\text{eq}} + B_\text{ref}(z)
\end{align*}
where $C$ is the total count for the image, $\hat P$ is the estimated particle contribution, and $B_\text{ref}(z)$ restores the reference particle rate with the appropriate angular-area scaling.
We include a coherent fractional error in the mock particle subtraction, with amplitude set by the counting uncertainty of the target's 9.5-12 keV background estimate.

We use a global source-brightness factor of 0.358, combining an initial catalogue-based factor of 0.278 with a further factor of 1.29 from the observed-to-mock count-rate ratio at approximately-matched mass and redshift.
The calibration does not use merger-time labels; however, the agreement in mean brightness values is partially imposed by this procedure.
For the 11 targets with weak-lensing masses, the corrected median real-to-mock rate ratio is 1.07, with no detected residual mass trend.

Target-dependent galactic absorption is applied using a count-rate ratio relative to the initial mock column of $N_\text{H} = 10^{20}$ cm$^{-2}$.
This ratio was calculated using an absorbed APEC spectrum with $kT = 5$ keV and metallicity $0.3 Z_\odot$, weighted by the Cycle-22 effective area.
This scalar approximation reduces the source count rate by approximately 2\% at the sample median.

We apply the same redshift-dependent aperture, exterior zero mask, and $\operatorname{arcsinh}(R/a)$ transform as for the observed images (section~\ref{sec:obs}), using one scale $a$ for both snapshots and all observations.

As a diagnostic, we measured the robust high-pass variance relative to the mean rate in the outer 60-100\% of the aperture radius, and compared the values for the mocks and real observations.
Across 14 targets with matched mocks, the median real-to-mock ratio was 1.12, with a 16th-84th percentile range of 0.81-1.20.
A1750 lacks a matched mock for this comparison.
A763 is an outlier, with a ratio of 0.07.

The mocks also do not reproduce each real observation's exposure footprint, roll angle, or full spectral response, and interpolation of gaps in the observed images can create correlated noise and altered variance.
