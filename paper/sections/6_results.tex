\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{What the radio image knows}
\begin{itemize}
  \item All at 144\,MHz, snapshot 99, 5 seeds, same folds. Pooled CNN
        $0.485 \pm 0.028$ (ensemble 0.530) vs total flux 0.440, mass 0.314,
        five scalars 0.459; scalars + CNN 0.541, an increment of $+0.082$.
  \item Within mass terciles: CNN 0.33/0.33/0.24 while scalars fall to
        $-0.29$ in the high-mass bin; partial $\rho = 0.480$ at fixed mass.
  \item Morphology alone (flux-normalised maps): $0.414 \pm 0.040$, paired
        $-0.071$ against full flux ($p = 0.019$, 5/5 seeds lower); but
        shape and brightness are not separable (60\% of the shape-only
        model's output is predictable from the total flux it never saw).
  \item Caveat: scalars explain 73\% of the CNN output.
  \item Minkowski functionals as an interpretable baseline: XGBoost on 48
        topology numbers reaches 0.42 (cluster mean of the three
        projections; 0.30 per projection); fragmentation rises with merger
        age ($\rho = 0.62$ for the component count at the highest
        threshold).
  \item The same analyses at 1.4\,GHz differ by at most 0.02 (the
        high-mass tercile, 0.22 there).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{X-ray, and radio with X-ray}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The stretch bug and fix: X-ray 0.320 $\to$ 0.511 ($p = 0.0003$).
  \item Joint model $0.537 \pm 0.016$: beats radio by $+0.050$ and X-ray by
        $+0.026$; gains on older mergers (RMSE $>2$\,Gyr 1.25 $\to$ 1.13).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Under observational degradation}
\label{sec:flux}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Anchored vs simulation-power flux (1.4\,GHz, snapshot 99):
        0.338 $\to$ 0.412; image beyond mass $+0.079 \to +0.151$;
        $\rho({\rm pred}, M)$ $-0.89 \to -0.61$.
  \item The simulation's $P_{150}$--\mfive, from the cells with the density
        cut: slope $2.67\pm0.16$, scatter 0.78\,dex. Compare the relic
        relation (de Gasperin et al.\ 2014, $2.83\pm0.39$), not the halo
        one (Cuciti et al.\ 2023, 3.55). Scatter tracks merger state with
        the observed sign. (The earlier 3.30 / 1.65\,dex came from an image
        approximation.)
  \item Realistic pipeline at 144\,MHz, with and without snapshot 91
        (Table~\ref{tab:realistic}). Adding the earlier epoch helps where
        the training images are noisiest -- X-ray and radio + mass
        significantly ($p \leq 0.04$, 5 paired seeds) -- and not at all for
        the clean radio CNN. Snapshot-91 clusters are themselves harder
        (0.35--0.43). $\rho({\rm pred}, M)$ strengthens slightly with
        snapshot 91.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Application to LoVoCCS}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Predictions for 25 (radio), 15 (X-ray), 14 (joint) clusters; all
        within the label range.
  \item Radio and X-ray agree (5-seed ensembles trained on snapshots
        99 + 91, 14 clusters): $\rho = 0.69$ ($p = 0.006$), 0.69 at fixed
        $z$; at fixed weak-lensing mass 0.48 ($n = 10$, $p = 0.16$).
        Snapshot 99 alone gives 0.61 / 0.66 / 0.52.
  \item Adding snapshot 91 keeps the real clusters' rank order
        ($\rho = 0.86$--0.95 against the snapshot-99 models) but shifts them
        0.1--0.25\,Gyr younger on average.
  \item A401 and A399 are still the youngest: A401 first of 25 for radio
        (and for radio + mass, and joint), A399 second for radio and joint
        and first for X-ray, with either training set. But A401 is also the
        heaviest LOFAR target with a weak-lensing mass, and a mass-only
        model puts it first too, so its youth is not evidence the images
        work. A399 has no weak-lensing mass, so its case cannot be tested
        this way. Do not present either as validation.
  \item Weak-lensing peak check: only 9 overlapping clusters,
        inconclusive.
\end{itemize}
