\subsection{Synchrotron emission from shock-accelerated electrons}
\label{sec:hybrid-radio}

We model shock-powered radio emission using the framework of \citet{HoeftBruggen2007}, with the electron energy
budget expressed in terms of the shock dissipation rate ($\dot E_{\rm diss}$) as in \citet{Lee2024}. Please refer to these two papers for details, but we will provide our key equations here.

Let $E$ be the electron kinetic energy and define
$\tilde e\equiv E/(m_ec^2)$, where $m_e$ is the electron mass and $c$ is
the speed of light. For a gas adiabatic index $\gamma_g=5/3$, the compression ratio $r$ and the spectral index $s$ of the accelerated electrons would be functions of Mach number $\mathcal M$, which we read directly from the snapshots of TNG-Cluster,
\begin{equation}
  s=\frac{r+2}{r-1}
   =\frac{2(\mathcal M^2+1)}{\mathcal M^2-1},
  \qquad
  r\equiv\frac{\rho_d}{\rho_u}
   =\frac{(\gamma_g+1)\mathcal M^2}
          {(\gamma_g-1)\mathcal M^2+2},
  \label{eq:radio-slope}
\end{equation}
where subscripts $u$
and $d$ denote upstream and downstream quantities. We adopt the electron spectrum generated by diffuse shock acceleration (DSA) with a high energy cutoff, similar to \citet{HoeftBruggen2007},
\begin{equation}
  n_E(E,0)\equiv\frac{dN_e}{dV\,dE}
  =\frac{\mathcal K}{m_ec^2}\,
   \tilde e^{-s}
   \left(1-\frac{\tilde e}{\tilde e_{\max}}\right)^{s-2},
  \qquad
  \tilde e_{\min}\leq\tilde e<\tilde e_{\max},
  \label{eq:radio-injection}
\end{equation}
and zero outside this interval. Here $\tilde e$ is $\frac{E}{m_ec^2}$ and $\mathcal K$ has units of number density and is fixed by the energy budget below. We set
\begin{equation}
  E_{\min}=10k_BT_d,
  \qquad
  \tilde e_{\min}=\frac{10k_BT_d}{m_ec^2},
  \qquad
  \tilde e_{\max}=10^7,
  \label{eq:radio-cutoffs}
\end{equation}
where $T_d$ is the downstream gas temperature. The lower cutoff is our
adopted approximation to the thermal--nonthermal matching prescription
of \citet{HoeftBruggen2007}; The assumed kinetic-energy power law is a phenomenological
continuation of the relativistic DSA spectrum to the adopted injection
threshold.

% Energy budget: HB eqs. (15)--(17); Lee eqs. (1) and (6).
For the shock patch,
\begin{equation}
  \dot E_{\rm diss}
  =A v_d\rho_d\Delta u_{\rm diss},
  \qquad
  \Delta u_{\rm diss}
  =u_d-u_u r^{\gamma_g-1},
  \label{eq:radio-dissipation}
\end{equation}
where $u_u$ and $u_d$ are the gas specific internal energies. A fraction
$\xi_e$ of this dissipated power is assigned to nonthermal electrons,
so that
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \xi_e\dot E_{\rm diss}
   &=A v_d\int_{E_{\min}}^{E_{\max}}
       E\,n_E(E,0)\,dE \\
   &=A v_d\mathcal K m_ec^2 I_{\rm spec}(s,T_d).
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:radio-energy-budget}
\end{equation}
We use the same notation as in \citet{HoeftBruggen2007} for an easier comparison. The dimensionless energy-normalization integral is
\begin{equation}
  I_{\rm spec}(s,T_d)
  =\int_{\tilde e_{\min}(T_d)}^{\tilde e_{\max}}
   \tilde e^{1-s}
   \left(1-\frac{\tilde e}{\tilde e_{\max}}\right)^{s-2}
   d\tilde e.
  \label{eq:radio-ispec}
\end{equation}
Thus $A v_d\mathcal K=\xi_e\dot E_{\rm diss}/
[m_ec^2 I_{\rm spec}(s,T_d)]$. We adopt $\xi_e=0.05$ as the fiducial
electron energy fraction, while retaining its explicit dependence below.

% Cooling: HB eqs. (18)--(24); Lee eqs. (4)--(5).
Following injection, the electrons advect downstream. At relativistic
energies their radiative evolution obeys
$d\tilde e/dt=-C_{\rm cool}\tilde e^2$, with
\begin{equation}
  C_{\rm cool}
  =\frac{\sigma_T}{6\pi m_ec}
    \left(B^2+B_{\rm CMB}^2\right),
  \qquad
  B_{\rm CMB}=3.24(1+z)^2\,\mu\mathrm{G},
  \label{eq:radio-cooling}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_T$ is the Thomson cross-section and $B_{\rm CMB}$ is the
magnetic-field equivalent of the cosmic microwave background energy
density. The cooled spectrum is
\begin{equation}
  n_E(E,t)=\frac{\mathcal K}{m_ec^2}\,
  \tilde e^{-s}
  \left[1-\left(\tilde e_{\max}^{-1}
               +C_{\rm cool}t\right)\tilde e\right]^{s-2}.
  \label{eq:radio-aged-spectrum}
\end{equation}
Here $t$ is the time since a fluid element crossed the shock. The
spectrum has support on the interval obtained by cooling the injected
energy limits,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\tilde e_{\min}}
       {1+C_{\rm cool}\tilde e_{\min}t}
  \leq\tilde e<
  \frac{\tilde e_{\max}}
       {1+C_{\rm cool}\tilde e_{\max}t},
  \label{eq:radio-cooled-support}
\end{equation}
and is zero otherwise within this cooling prescription.

% Downstream integral: HB eq. (29); Lee eq. (7).
For a uniform patch, the spectral luminosity $L_\nu\equiv dP/d\nu$ is
obtained by integrating the single-electron spectral power over electron
energy and downstream distance. Since electrons that have cooled below
$E_{\min}$ contribute negligibly at the radio frequencies considered,
we write
\begin{equation}
  \frac{L_\nu}{A}
  \simeq\int_0^\infty dy
  \int_{E_{\min}}^{E_{\max}}dE\,
  n_E(E,t=y/v_d)\,P_e(\nu,E).
  \label{eq:radio-surface-integral}
\end{equation}
The distance--age relation $y=v_dt$ converts this spatial integral into
an integral over electron age. It sums the emission of downstream fluid
elements present at the same source time.

% Single-electron emission: HB eqs. (25)--(26); Lee eq. (8).
Following the approximate pitch-angle treatment of
\citet{HoeftBruggen2007}, we replace $\sin\theta$ by
$\langle\sin\theta\rangle=\pi/4$. The single-electron spectral power is
then
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  P_e(\nu,E)&=C_{\rm sync}F\!\left(\frac{\nu}{\nu_c}\right),
  & C_{\rm sync}&=\frac{\sqrt{3}\pi e^3B}{4m_ec^2},\\
  F(x)&=x\int_x^\infty K_{5/3}(w)\,dw,
  & \nu_c&=\frac{3eB}{16m_ec}\gamma_L^2,
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:radio-single-electron}
\end{equation}
where $e$ is the magnitude of the electron charge, $K_{5/3}$ is a
modified Bessel function, and $\gamma_L=1+\tilde e$. For the
radio-emitting electrons, $\gamma_L\simeq\tilde e\gg1$. Introducing
\begin{equation}
  C_\tau=\left(\frac{3eB}{16m_ec\nu}\right)^{1/2},
  \qquad \tau=C_\tau\tilde e,
  \qquad \frac{\nu}{\nu_c}\simeq\tau^{-2},
  \label{eq:radio-ctau}
\end{equation}
makes the frequency dependence explicit.

% Age integration: derived from HB eq. (21) / Lee eq. (4).
At fixed $E_{\min}\leq E<E_{\max}$ the spectrum vanishes after
$t_{\max}=(1-\tilde e/\tilde e_{\max})/
(C_{\rm cool}\tilde e)$. Integrating
Eq.~\eqref{eq:radio-aged-spectrum} over age therefore gives
\begin{equation}
  \int_0^\infty n_E(E,t)\,dt
  =\frac{\mathcal K}{m_ec^2 C_{\rm cool}(s-1)}
   \tilde e^{-s-1}
   \left(1-\frac{\tilde e}{\tilde e_{\max}}\right)^{s-1}.
  \label{eq:radio-age-integral}
\end{equation}
We assume that the energies dominating the radio emission lie well
inside the injection interval,
$\tilde e_{\min}\ll\tilde e_{\rm radio}\ll\tilde e_{\max}$.
We can then neglect the cutoff factor in the radiation integral and
extend its limits to zero and infinity. The finite cutoffs are retained
in $I_{\rm spec}$, because they set the normalization of the injected
electron energy. Substituting Eqs.~\eqref{eq:radio-single-electron}--
\eqref{eq:radio-age-integral} into Eq.~\eqref{eq:radio-surface-integral}
and using $dE=m_ec^2d\tilde e$ yields
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  L_\nu
  &\simeq A v_d\mathcal K\,
       \frac{C_{\rm sync}}{C_{\rm cool}}
       C_\tau^s I_\Psi(s)\\
  &=\frac{\xi_e\dot E_{\rm diss}}{m_ec^2}
    \frac{C_{\rm sync}}{C_{\rm cool}}
    C_\tau^s\frac{I_\Psi(s)}{I_{\rm spec}(s,T_d)}.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:radio-unscaled-luminosity}
\end{equation}
Here the remaining dimensionless radiation integral, corresponding to
the integral in Eq.~(31) of \citet{HoeftBruggen2007} after the age
integration, is
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  I_\Psi(s)
  &\equiv\frac{1}{s-1}\int_0^\infty
      \tau^{-s-1}F(\tau^{-2})\,d\tau\\
  &=\frac{2^{s/2}}{(s-1)(s+2)}
    \Gamma\!\left(\frac{s}{4}+\frac{11}{6}\right)
    \Gamma\!\left(\frac{s}{4}+\frac{1}{6}\right),
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:radio-ipsi}
\end{equation}
where $\Gamma$ is the Euler gamma function. The factors $A$, $v_d$, and
the gas energy density have consequently been absorbed into
$\dot E_{\rm diss}$.

% Our reference normalization and Eq. (9)-type parametrization.
To express Eq.~\eqref{eq:radio-unscaled-luminosity} in the form of
Eq.~(9) of \citet{Lee2024}, we set $B_0=1\,\mu\mathrm{G}$ and
$\nu_0=1.4\,\mathrm{GHz}$ and define
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  C_{{\rm sync},0}&=\frac{\sqrt{3}\pi e^3B_0}{4m_ec^2},\\
  C_{{\rm cool},0}&=\frac{\sigma_T B_0^2}{6\pi m_ec},\\
  C_{\tau,0}&=\left(\frac{3eB_0}{16m_ec\nu_0}\right)^{1/2}
             \simeq4.8534\times10^{-5}.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:radio-reference-coefficients}
\end{equation}
In particular, $C_{{\rm cool},0}$ excludes the CMB contribution; the
full cooling coefficient is
$C_{\rm cool}=C_{{\rm cool},0}
[(B/B_0)^2+(B_{\rm CMB}/B_0)^2]$.

We normalize the residual spectral factor at $s_*=2$, corresponding to
$\mathcal M\rightarrow\infty$, and at $k_BT_*=7\,\mathrm{keV}$. For the
adopted cutoffs,
\begin{equation}
  I_*\equiv I_{\rm spec}(2,T_*)
  =\ln\!\left[\frac{\tilde e_{\max}}
                    {\tilde e_{\min}(T_*)}\right]
  \simeq18.106,
  \qquad
  I_\Psi(2)=\frac{4\pi}{9\sqrt{3}}.
  \label{eq:radio-reference-integrals}
\end{equation}
The dimensionless spectral factor is defined by
\begin{equation}
  \Phi(\mathcal M,T_d)
  \equiv C_{\tau,0}^{\,s(\mathcal M)-2}
    \frac{I_\Psi[s(\mathcal M)]}{I_\Psi(2)}
    \frac{I_*}{I_{\rm spec}[s(\mathcal M),T_d]}.
  \label{eq:radio-phi}
\end{equation}
Taking $\xi_*=0.05$ and
$\dot E_*=10^{44}\,\mathrm{erg\,s^{-1}}$, the associated dimensional
coefficient is
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  P_*&\equiv
  \frac{\xi_*\dot E_*}{m_ec^2}
  \frac{C_{{\rm sync},0}}{C_{{\rm cool},0}}
  C_{\tau,0}^{2}\frac{I_\Psi(2)}{I_*}\\
  &\simeq9.13\times10^{31}\,
        \mathrm{erg\,s^{-1}\,Hz^{-1}}\\
  &=9.13\times10^{24}\,\mathrm{W\,Hz^{-1}}.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:radio-pstar}
\end{equation}
The resulting spectral luminosity is
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{
  \begin{aligned}
  L_\nu\simeq{}&P_*
   \left(\frac{\xi_e}{0.05}\right)
   \left(\frac{\dot E_{\rm diss}}
                   {10^{44}\,\mathrm{erg\,s^{-1}}}\right)\\
  &\times
   \frac{(B/\mu\mathrm{G})^{1+s/2}}
        {(B/\mu\mathrm{G})^2+(B_{\rm CMB}/\mu\mathrm{G})^2}
   \left(\frac{\nu}{1.4\,\mathrm{GHz}}\right)^{-s/2}
   \Phi(\mathcal M,T_d).
  \end{aligned}}
  \label{eq:radio-final}
\end{equation}
The temperature dependence of $\Phi$ arises from the lower injection
cutoff in $I_{\rm spec}$. By construction,
$\Phi(\mathcal M\rightarrow\infty,T_*)=1$, whereas at a different
temperature
\begin{equation}
  \Phi(\mathcal M\rightarrow\infty,T_d)
  =\frac{I_*}
  {\ln[\tilde e_{\max}/\tilde e_{\min}(T_d)]}.
  \label{eq:radio-strong-limit}
\end{equation}
The reference temperature fixes the separate definitions of $P_*$ and
$\Phi$; changing it consistently leaves their product, and hence the
predicted luminosity, unchanged. We evaluate the coefficient directly
from the physical constants and the adopted spectral and pitch-angle.

\subsection{X-ray mocks}

We generated synthetic X-ray observations using the package \textsc{pyXSIM}, with an Astrophysical Plasma Emission Code (APEC) set to be in collisional ionization equilibrium.
The emitting gas was selected within a cube of side length $2 R_{500\text{c}}$ centered on the FoF position, using the halo properties at the selected snapshot.
We retain gas with $T > 3 \times 10^5$ K, zero star formation rate, and \texttt{GFM\_CoolingRate} $<0$.
The source model uses the simulated gas temperatures, metallicities, and emission measures.

For each cluster, photons were projected along each of three orthogonal directions corresponding to the three radio projections.
The snapshot-99 clusters are placed at an observing redshift of $z_{\text{obs}} = 0.05$, while the snapshot-91 clusters are observed at $z_\text{obs} = 0.0994$.
We use photoelectric absorption using the \texttt{wabs} model, with a uniform hydrogen column density of $N_\text{H}= 10^{20}$ cm$^2$.
The photons were then processed using the \textsc{SOXS} Chandra ACIS-I Cycle-22 instrument model, using an exposure time of 2 Ms.
These observations take into account the instrument response, point-spread function, detector geometry, dithering, and instrumental particle background.
Galactic foreground and point-source backgrounds were disabled when generating these initial mocks, and were added during the forward modelling in section \ref{sec:fwd}.

We constructed exposure-corrected images in the observed 0.1--2.0 keV band using exposure maps evaluated at 1.2 keV.
The native images have a pixel scale of 0.492".
For snapshot-99, we crop the images to $128\times 128$ pixels, which corresponds to approximately 18.9 kpc per pixel.
The ACIS-I field has a radius of approximately 1 Mpc at $z_\text{obs} = 0.05$, so the usable coverage is set by the detector, rather than the available image data.

In the subsequent forward-modelling step, we recover integer count estimates from the exposure-corrected images.
The snapshot-91 images were placed on the same physical grid as snapshot-99 by binomially allocating each native pixel's counts between overlapping output pixels in proportion to their overlap areas.
This procedure preserves integer counts, although their recovery is approximate where the original exposure varies across chip gaps and dither edges.

Before training, we compress the image dynamic range using $\operatorname{arcsinh}(x/a)$, where $x$ is the image signal and $a$ is a single scale factor used for each image from both snapshots.
The adjustments to target redshift, exposure, luminosity, absorption, and sky background are described in section \ref{sec:xray}.

% We characterize the merger state of each target halo using the time since collision (TSC). Using the major merger catalog of \cite{Lee2024}, we identify the major merger whose first pericenter passage is closest in time to the snapshot of interest. We then define the TSC label as
% \begin{equation}

Our primary regression target is the \emph{pseudo-TSC}, a proxy for recent merger activity constructed from the precomputed TNG-Cluster merger scores.
For each snapshot and halo, the merger score was sampled with window parameters $\tau = 0.1, 0.2, \ldots 4.0$ Gyr.
The pseudo-TSC is defined as the value of $\tau$ at which the merger score first reaches 0.5, using linear interpolation between the two samples which bracket the threshold value:
\begin{equation}
    T_\text{pseudo}
    = \tau_{j-1} + 
     \frac{0.5-S(\tau_{j-1})}
       {S(\tau_j)-S(\tau_{j-1})}
  (\tau_j-\tau_{j-1}),
\end{equation}
where $j$ is the first sampled window with $S(\tau_j) \geq 0.5$ and $S(\tau_i)$ is the merger score at $\tau_i$.
If the merger score is already at the 0.5 threshold at the smallest window, we assign $T_\text{pseudo} = 0.1$ Gyr.
If there is no crossing within the sampled range, we assign $T_\text{pseudo} = 4$ Gyr.
